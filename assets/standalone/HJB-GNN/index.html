Learning Distributed Safe Multi-Agent Navigation via Infinite-Horizon Optimal Graph Control

Fenglan Wang, Xinguo Shu, Lei He, Lin Zhao*
National University of Singapore
Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering
*Corresponding Author

Abstract

Distributed multi-agent navigation faces inherent challenges due to the competing requirements of maintaining safety and achieving goal-directed behavior, especially for agents with limited sensing range and in environments with dense obstacles. Traditional navigation methods typically employ hand-crafted control barrier functions (CBFs) alongside pre-defined goal-reaching controllers, which often yield conservative and suboptimal trade-offs between safety and goal reaching. This paper proposes a novel physics-informed learning framework for distributed safe multi-agent navigation. Specifically, we formulate infinite-horizon Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations subject to graph CBF-based safety constraints and employ graph neural networks (GNNs) to parameterize both a graph CBF and a distributed control policy. These components are jointly trained alongside a value function that robustly guides agents toward their goals. In addition, we introduce a state-dependent parameterization of Lagrange multipliers, enabling dynamic trade-offs between safety and performance. Unlike traditional short-horizon, quadratic programming-based CBF methods, our approach leverages long-horizon optimization to proactively avoid deadlocks and navigate complex environments more effectively. Extensive simulation results demonstrate substantial improvements in safety and task success rates across various agent dynamics, with strong generalization to larger team sizes. Real-world experiments using crazyflie drone swarms on challenging antipodal position-swapping tasks further validate the practicality, scalability, and robustness of the proposed HJB-GNN framework.

Validation Simulations

Fig. 1. Comparison between QP-GCBF+ of [31] in (a) and the proposed HJB-GNN in (b). An agent (a gradient-colored circle) aims to reach the goal (blue rectangle) while avoiding static obstacles (gray rectangles) within a \(4{\rm m}\times4{\rm m}\) region. The black arrow indicates the velocity direction of the agent. The QP-GCBF+ has deadlock near obstacles, and the HJB-GNN approach achieves goal-reaching without collision.

Fig. 2. Illustration of our HJB-GNN approach on the unmanned surface vessel under 4 agents and 4 static obstacles case in a \(2\,\mathrm{m} \times 1.5\,\mathrm{m}\) region: (a) four agents (circles) reach their respective goals (rectangles with the same color as agents) while avoiding collisions with other agents and static obstacles (gray rectangles), (b) graph CBF contour of agent 2 at \(t=0.6{\rm s}\), (c) approximated Lagrange multipliers \(\hat{\lambda}_i,\ i=1,2,3,4\) of four agents, and (d) distributed controller \(\pi_{\varphi}(\eta_2)=[\pi_{\varphi 1}(\eta_2),\pi_{\varphi 2}(\eta_2),\pi_{\varphi 3}(\eta_2)]^T\) of agent 2.

Fig. 3. Safe-reaching rates ((a), (c), (e)) and safety rates ((b), (d), (f)) for 2D unmanned surface vessel under fixed area width. (a)-(b): no obstacle with increasing number of agents, (c)-(d): 256 agents with increasing number of obstacles, and (e)-(f): 1024 agents with increasing number of obstacles.
