Safe Distributed Control of Multi-Agent Systems: An Infinite-Horizon Optimization-Guided Graph Learning Approach

Fenglan Wang, Xinguo Shu, Lei He, Lin Zhao*
National University of Singapore
Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering
*Corresponding Author

Abstract

Safe distributed control for arbitrary sized multi-agent systems remains challenging, especially under limited sensing radius and arbitrary time-varying neighborhoods. Existing control barrier function (CBF)-based safety methods often depend on hand-crafted CBFs, nominal controllers for task performance, or manually tuned static safety and task performance trade-offs, which may reduce scalability and generalizability. To avoid these issues, in this paper, a novel infinite-horizon optimization-guided graph learning (IHO-GL) approach is proposed for safe distributed control of nonlinear multi-agent systems under limited sensing radius and arbitrary time-varying neighborhoods, where each agent can safety reach its goal position. In the proposed approach, single distributed CBF, that only depends on the information of agent and its neighbors, is embedded into constrained Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations. Solving these equations via KKT conditions yields a unified theoretical framework for ensuring both safety and goal-reaching performance for arbitrary sized multi-agent systems with single distributed CBF, without the nominal controllers. Based on this, for scalability and generalizability, the proposed IHO-GL approach learns one distributed CBF and one safe distributed controller using graph neural networks. One value function is parameterized by a separate non-graph neural networks due to independent of neighboring information. In addition, the state-dependent Lagrange multipliers further achieve dynamic trade-offs between safety and goal-reaching performance, enhancing both scalability and generalizability. Finally, extensive numerical simulations demonstrate that the IHO-GL approach achieves high safety rates and safe-reaching rates while effectively improving scalability and generalizability in cluttered environments. The practical applicability is further validated by performing four sets of hardware experiments on a swarm of Crazyflie drones under antipodal position swapping task.

Validation Simulations

Fig. 1. Comparison between QP-GCBF+ of [4] in (a) and the proposed IHO-GL in (b). An agent (a gradient-colored circle) aims to reach the goal (blue rectangle) while avoiding static obstacles (gray rectangles) within a \(4{\rm m}\times4{\rm m}\) region. The black arrow indicates the velocity direction of the agent. The QP-GCBF+ has deadlock near obstacles, and the IHO-GL approach achieves goal-reaching without collision.

Fig. 2. Illustration of the IHO-GL approach on the unmanned surface vessel under 4 agents and 4 static obstacles case in a \(2\,\mathrm{m} \times 1.5\,\mathrm{m}\) region: (a) four agents (circles) reach their respective goals (rectangles with the same color as agents) while avoiding collisions with other agents and static obstacles (gray rectangles), (b) distributed CBF contour of agent 2 at \(t=0.6{\rm s}\), (c) approximated Lagrange multipliers \(\hat{\lambda}_i,\ i=1,2,3,4\) of four agents, and (d) distributed controller \(\pi_{\varphi}(\eta_2)=[\pi_{\varphi 1}(\eta_2),\pi_{\varphi 2}(\eta_2),\pi_{\varphi 3}(\eta_2)]^T\) of agent 2.

Fig. 3. Safe-reaching rates ((a), (c), (e)) and safety rates ((b), (d), (f)) for 2-D unmanned surface vessel under fixed area width. (a)-(b): no obstacle with an increasing number of agents, (c)-(d): 256 agents with an increasing number of obstacles, and (e)-(f): 1024 agents with an increasing number of obstacles.
